What a useful audit looks at

A checklist of settings is easy to produce and rarely changes anything. The things below are where the money usually is, and every finding is tied back to what it means for your budget, your targets or your next decision.

  • Measurement. Which conversions bidding is optimising towards, whether the values are right, and how consent mode and enhanced conversions affect what you see.
  • Targets and economics. Whether your ROAS or CPA targets reflect margin and lifetime value, or a number nobody has revisited.
  • Brand and non-brand. How much of the reported return is people who were already searching for you.
  • Performance Max and AI Max. Where automated campaigns are spending, what they add and where they overlap with Search.
  • Search terms and waste. What you're really paying for, and the negatives and exclusions that are missing.
  • Budget headroom. Which campaigns are capped by budget, which are held back by rank, and which have simply run out of good demand.

Free agency audit or independent audit?

Both have their place. It helps to know what each one is for.

A free agency audit

Usually part of a pitch. It can be genuinely useful, but it's written by someone who would like to manage the account, so the findings tend to point towards switching.

An automated checker

Fast and free, and good at spotting settings. It can't see your margins, your sales team or what you're trying to decide, so it can't tell you which findings matter.

An independent audit

Paid for by you and written for you. The scope is your question, the findings are ranked by what they're worth, and it's fine to share it with your agency.
