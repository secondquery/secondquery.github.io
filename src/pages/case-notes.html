The decision

The account had been inherited underperforming. The question was whether to keep patching the old structure or rebuild it around how people actually ordered: on the website and, increasingly, in the app.

What I did

  • Rebuilt the account into a simpler, modern structure covering Search, Performance Max and App campaigns, each with a clear job at its stage of the funnel.
  • Made sure online orders and in-app orders were both counted, so bidding could follow real transactions rather than clicks.
  • Lined search up with the brand's YouTube activity, so the demand video created was captured in search rather than left to chance.

What changed

Transactions grew roughly in line with spend, but the orders were worth far more: average order value rose 225% and conversion rate 167%. That took revenue up 402% and return on ad spend up 216%, with app installs up 78%.

Compared: 10 June to 31 October 2024 against the previous period of equal length. Spend also rose and the seasons differ, so read this as what changed after the rebuild, not a controlled test.

The decision

Paid search was missing its return target, and the SEO and paid teams disagreed about cannibalisation: was the business paying for clicks it would have won organically anyway? Meanwhile, large parts of the range had no paid coverage at all.

What I did

  • Restructured the account with a full negative keyword framework, so each search landed in the campaign built for it.
  • Separated core brand searches and stopped bidding on them, letting the organic listings capture that traffic.
  • Put the money saved into broader, non-brand searches across the parts of the range paid search hadn't reached.

What changed

On 17% less spend, paid search drove 114% more transactions and 134% more revenue, with conversion rate up 155% and average order value up 10%. Across paid and organic together, return on spend rose 82% and revenue 57%, because the brand traffic moved to organic rather than disappearing.

Compared: paid results for August to October 2024 against May to July 2024; the combined paid and organic figures compare five weeks after the brand change with the five weeks before it. Different seasons again, so the combined view, which accounts for traffic moving between channels, is the fairer read.

The decision

The accounts were spending inefficiently and reaching too few of the people the brands wanted. The goal was to reach more engaged users without the cost running away.

What I did

  • Consolidated a fragmented structure into fewer, stronger campaigns, so each had enough data to work with.
  • Added conversion points to the relevant campaigns, giving automated bidding something meaningful to aim at.
  • Moved the conversion-focused campaigns to broad match, steered by those conversion signals, to widen reach to relevant searches.

What changed

As the budget was scaled up, clicks rose 7.5 times and click-through rate 4.2 times, while cost per click fell 25%. More of the right people saw and chose the ads, and each visit cost less.

Compared: April to August 2023 against November 2022 to March 2023. Budget increased over the period, which is part of the click growth; the cost per click and click-through rate show the efficiency.
