Privacy notice

Short version: this site only sets cookies if you accept them, the only cookie is Google Ads measuring which ads lead to enquiries, and I only use your details to reply to you and do the work you ask for.

Last updated: 21 November 2026

Who I am

Second Query is the trading name of Sam Hitchmough, a sole trader. I'm the controller of the personal information described here. You can contact me at {{email}}.

This website

To count visits, this site uses Cloudflare Web Analytics, which doesn't use cookies or track individual visitors. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, so those providers will see technical information such as your IP address when your browser requests the page. When you send the brief form, what you type goes to my Google Workspace account, where it's emailed to me and logged in a private Google Sheet, and you get a copy by email.

Cookies

Nothing is set on your device unless you choose Accept on the cookie banner. If you accept, the Google Ads tag sets first-party cookies (such as _gcl_au and _gcl_aw) for up to 90 days so Google Ads can count an enquiry that came from one of my ads. Personalised advertising is switched off, and there are no analytics or social media cookies. If you decline, the site works exactly the same. Your choice is saved in your browser for six months, and you can change it at any time with Cookie settings, which also removes the Google Ads cookies.

What I collect and why

  • When you contact me: your name, work email, role, company and whatever you include in your message. I use it to reply and, if you go ahead, to scope and deliver the work. The lawful basis is taking steps at your request before a contract, and then performing the contract.
  • When you're a client: contact details, invoices and the account data you choose to share for the engagement. The lawful bases are contract, and legal obligation for financial records.
  • If you arrived from one of my ads: the ad network adds a click reference (such as a Google click ID) to the page address. If, and only if, you accept cookies, the Google tag stores that reference and tells Google Ads when you send a brief, and I keep the reference with your brief so I can later tell Google Ads whether the enquiry became a client. It's used to measure which ads work, not for profiling you or showing you ads elsewhere. The lawful basis is your consent, which you can withdraw at any time using Cookie settings at the bottom of any page.
  • Business outreach: I sometimes contact people at companies, using work contact details that are publicly available, about services that are relevant to their role. The lawful basis is legitimate interests. Every message explains how to opt out, and if you do, I'll keep a minimal record so I don't contact you again.

The Account X-ray

The Account X-ray reads the report you choose in your own browser. Your file isn't uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.

Client account data

For reviews I use read-only access or exports you choose to share. I ask for the minimum data needed, I don't use one client's data for another client, and I remove access and delete working data within 30 days of the engagement ending, unless you ask me to keep it.

Who I share it with

I don't sell personal information or share it for marketing. I use a small number of service providers to run the business, including Google Workspace for email, documents and the brief form, Google Ads and Microsoft Advertising (ad measurement, only if you accept cookies), GitHub for this website, Cloudflare for cookie-free visit counts, and my bank and accountant for invoicing and tax. They process data on my behalf under their own terms.

Where it's stored

Information is stored with the providers above, who may process it outside the UK using recognised safeguards such as the UK International Data Transfer Addendum. I may also access it while working or travelling outside the UK.

How long I keep it

  • Enquiries that don't go ahead: up to 12 months.
  • Client records and invoices: six years after the end of the tax year they relate to, to meet UK record-keeping rules.
  • Opt-out records: for as long as needed to respect your choice.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your information, ask me to correct or delete it, object to how I use it or ask me to restrict it. To do any of these, email {{email}}. I'll reply within one month.

If you're unhappy with how I've handled your information, please tell me first. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
