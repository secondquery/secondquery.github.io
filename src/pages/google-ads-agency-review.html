Is your Google Ads agency doing a good job?

It's one of the hardest questions in marketing to answer from the inside. The reports look fine, the agency is confident, and yet the results don't quite match what finance sees. Here's how to check, and when it's worth asking someone independent.

Signs worth checking yourself

None of these proves anything on its own. Together they tell you whether a closer look is worth paying for.

  • Reporting leads with platform numbers. If "conversions" and ROAS from Google Ads never get reconciled with your own sales or CRM, nobody really knows what's working.
  • Brand isn't separated out. Brand campaigns usually look brilliant. If they're blended with everything else, the account can look healthier than it is.
  • Targets haven't moved in a year. ROAS or CPA targets should follow margin, stock and lifetime value, not stay frozen.
  • New features are switched on without a conversation. Performance Max, AI Max and automatically created assets can be useful, but the decision to use them should be yours.
  • Recommendations always mean more budget. Sometimes that's right. If it's always the answer, ask where the extra money would go and what it would return.
  • You can't get a straight answer on search terms. You're entitled to see what you're paying for.

Is it the agency, or the set-up?

Often it's neither on its own. These are the usual places a good agency gets held back.

The brief

Agencies optimise towards what they're asked for. If the goal is "more leads" and the business needs better leads, the account will deliver the wrong thing very efficiently.

The data

If conversion tracking, values or consent mode aren't right, Smart Bidding is learning from the wrong signals, however good the people are.

The ways of working

Slow approvals, unclear ownership and monthly-only check-ins make it hard for anyone to act on what the data says.

Questions marketing leaders ask before reviewing their agency.

Should we tell our agency about an independent review?
Usually, yes. It gets better data and a more honest conversation, and a good agency will welcome a clear, evidence-based view. The review is written so it can be shared with them.
Will the review just say we should change agency?
No. I don't run accounts, so I gain nothing from a switch. Plenty of reviews conclude that the agency is doing a solid job and that the brief, the targets or the measurement need to change.
