Sample Decision Review

This is what you'd get from a £1,750 Decision Review, written the way a real one is. The business, the people and every number are invented, and no client data is used. It's here so you can judge the format and the thinking before you buy one.

Prepared for

The marketing director and board of Ashby & Vale Windows (invented)

The business

A UK windows and doors installer. Leads come from a quote form and phone calls, then a home survey, then a signed order. Average order about £6,800. Most orders are signed within a month of the first enquiry.

Google Ads

£42,000 a month, run by an agency. Their target is a cost per lead of £60.

Data used

Read-only Google Ads access, a CRM export of leads and signed orders, and finance's ceiling of £700 per signed order.

The question agreed on the first call

"The agency wants an extra £15,000 a month for the spring peak, mostly in Performance Max. Should the board approve it?"

1. The answer

Approve £6,000 of the £15,000 now, and put it into Search, not Performance Max. Decide on the other £9,000 in week 11, once you can see signed orders rather than leads.

Two Search campaigns, Windows and Doors, are held back by budget while signing orders at around half of finance's £700 ceiling. That's the safest place for new money. Performance Max hits the agency's £60 lead target, but on the orders we can match it costs about £1,280 per signed order. Until it's bidding on orders, more budget there is more likely to buy cheap leads than sales.

Keep the agency. They have done what they were asked: hit a cost per lead. The problem is that leads aren't what the business is paid for. Changing the target they work to is a brief change, not a reason to switch agency.

How sure I am: fairly sure about the £6,000, less sure about Performance Max. Phone calls are matched to orders much less often than forms, and Performance Max drives more calls, so it may be doing better than the numbers show. Section 3 explains, and the plan fixes that gap before the bigger decision.

2. The evidence

Leads from August and September, which have all had at least 30 days to turn into orders. In your CRM history, about 85% of orders are signed within 30 days of the lead, so these counts are close to complete.

Why these two campaigns. Windows and Doors are the only non-brand campaigns that pass all three tests: capped by budget, under the £700 ceiling, and with enough orders to read (39 and 21). Repairs isn't capped, and three orders is too few to judge.

Why £6,000, not £15,000. At today's cost per click, winning back the impressions these two campaigns lose to budget would take about £5,000 a month. Clicks cost more at the margin, so £6,000 covers most of that headroom. Beyond it, extra Search budget has nowhere useful to go, which is the honest limit of this recommendation.

Why not Performance Max yet. Search terms insights suggest about 35% of its conversions come from brand-style searches. Together with brand Search, that means around 41% of all reported conversions involve people already looking for you. Performance Max's non-brand cost per order is probably higher than £1,282, but it can't be measured cleanly until brand traffic is excluded.

3. What I couldn't check, and what would change my view

  • Calls are under-matched. Calls are 31% of leads, but only 52% of calls can be matched to a CRM record, against 88% of forms. Performance Max drives a bigger share of calls (44%, against 22% for Search). If fixing call matching brings Performance Max under £700 per order, it becomes the right home for the second £9,000.
  • Orders are still arriving. About 15% of orders come after 30 days. That nudges every cost per order down a little, but it doesn't change the order of the campaigns.
  • Autumn isn't spring. The baseline is August and September, and the money is for the spring peak. The test below uses Repairs and brand Search as a comparison, so a market-wide rise isn't mistaken for the effect of the budget.
  • Lead quality by region. Survey and installation capacity varies by region, and I haven't seen it. If some regions are already full, extra leads there won't become orders.

4. The test, and when to stop

One change at a time, read on signed orders, with a stop rule agreed before it starts.

The change

Add £6,000 a month to Windows and Doors from week 3. Budget only: same bid strategy, same targets, same ads.

Early warning (week 5)

If weekly leads from the two campaigns rise less than 15%, the money isn't buying new auctions. Stop early.

The read (week 11)

Leads from weeks 3 to 6, each with 30 days to become orders. Extra orders are measured against the two campaigns' August to September rate, adjusted by how much Repairs and brand moved in the same weeks.

The decision rule

Extra spend ÷ extra orders. £700 or less: keep it and consider the next tranche. £700 to £900: hold for four more weeks. Over £900: return budgets to where they were.

A caveat on small numbers: at £700 an order, £6,000 a month needs about nine extra orders to pass. That's a small number to read, which is why this is a staged decision rather than a single yes or no.

5. Risks

  • Spring costs per click rise. That would make the extra spend look worse than it is. The Repairs and brand comparison catches most of this.
  • Lead counts fall when Performance Max moves to orders. That's expected: fewer, better leads. Agree with the board now that the agency's lead target is being replaced, so nobody reads the drop as failure.
  • Brand exclusions cut Performance Max volume. Some of those conversions would have arrived through brand Search anyway. Watch brand Search leads in the same weeks to see how many come back.

6. The 90-day plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: fix what we judge by. Capture the click ID on calls, start importing signed orders and their values into Google Ads, and exclude brand from Performance Max. No budget changes yet.
  2. Weeks 3 to 10: the £6,000 test. Windows and Doors only, with the early-warning check in week 5.
  3. Week 11: read and decide. Apply the rule above. If call matching is fixed and Performance Max is bidding on orders, compare it against Search for the remaining £9,000.
  4. Weeks 11 to 13: the agency brief. Replace the £60 lead target with a cost per signed order, using the imported order data.

What I'd tell the board, in one paragraph

Approve £6,000 a month now, not £15,000. It goes where the evidence is strongest: two Search campaigns that sign orders at about half our ceiling and are held back by budget. The rest waits ten weeks, until we can see orders, not leads, for Performance Max. We keep the agency and change what we ask them to hit. If the extra spend costs more than £900 per order by week 11, it comes back out.
