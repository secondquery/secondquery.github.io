Match types for good advice

The same discipline that keeps a good account clean keeps advice useful: be precise about the question, strict about evidence and clear about what's out of scope.

Exact match
[one decision]

Every engagement starts by writing down the decision in one sentence. Findings that don't change that decision go in an appendix, not the headline.

Phrase match
"evidence first"

Each recommendation points to the data behind it, says how confident I am and says what would change my mind. No "best practice" for its own sake.

Broad match, with good signals
whole business

Google Ads doesn't live on its own. Margins, stock, sales capacity, brand demand and other channels all shape what "good" looks like.

Questions I ask of every account

Most accounts don't fail on settings. They fail because the platform is optimising towards the wrong thing, or nobody can see clearly enough to tell. These are the questions that usually move the decision.

  • Is bidding optimising towards profit, or towards something that only looks like it?
  • How much of the reported return is brand demand you'd win anyway?
  • Where is automation helping, and where is it hiding the detail you need?
  • Which campaigns are limited by budget, and which have simply run out of good demand?
  • Do targets reflect margin and lifetime value, or last year's spreadsheet?
  • Who owns which decision, and how quickly do changes actually happen?

How your data is handled

Access to an ad account is a privilege. It's treated like one.

Read-only access

A read-only user on Google Ads, or exports you choose. I never make changes, launch anything or touch billing.

Only what's needed

I ask for the minimum data the decision needs, and I'm happy to sign your NDA before we start.

Removed afterwards

Access is removed at the end of the engagement, and working data is deleted within 30 days unless you ask me to keep it.

Working with your agency or team

The best Reviews make the people running the account look good, because they give them a clear mandate.

Invited, not ambushed

I'd rather your agency or team knows a Review is happening. It gets better data and a better conversation.

Specific, not sweeping

Findings are written so the people doing the work can act on them on Monday morning, not as a list of grades.

Credit where it's due

When something is working well, the Review says so plainly, so it doesn't get changed by accident.

Start with the decision

Write it in a sentence and send it over. That's usually enough for a useful first call.
