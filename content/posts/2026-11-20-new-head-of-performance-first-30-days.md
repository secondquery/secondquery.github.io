Starting a new performance role is a strange mix of pressure and blindness. Everyone wants to know your plan, and you've inherited an account you didn't build, run by people you've just met, measured in ways you haven't checked yet.

The temptation is to change things quickly to show momentum. In Google Ads, that's usually a mistake. Changes reset learning, muddy the baseline and make it harder to tell whether you inherited a problem or created one.

These are the six questions I'd want answered in the first month, before changing anything.

## 1. What is the account actually optimising towards?

Look at the primary conversion actions and the values attached to them. Is bidding chasing sales, qualified leads or something easier to count, like form starts? Are values real numbers, or placeholders someone set years ago?

This one question explains more performance problems than any other.

## 2. How much of the result is brand?

Split performance into brand and non-brand across Search and Performance Max. Brand campaigns often carry the headline return, and much of that demand would arrive anyway. Knowing the real non-brand picture changes almost every budget conversation.

## 3. Where is the budget capped, and where has demand run out?

Some campaigns are limited by budget and would grow with more money. Others have already found most of the good demand, and extra spend would just push returns down. You'll be asked for "more growth" in your first quarter. This is how you know where it can come from.

## 4. What did the last six months of changes look like?

Read the change history. Lots of target changes, structural rebuilds or new campaign types in a short period often explain unstable results better than any market factor.

## 5. Do the numbers agree with the business?

Compare Google Ads conversions with what the CRM or finance team sees for the same period. A gap is normal. A gap nobody can explain is a measurement problem, and it should be fixed before anything is optimised against it.

## 6. Who decides what?

Find out who can change budgets, targets and campaign types, how quickly changes happen and how decisions are recorded. Many accounts underperform not because of the strategy but because nobody owns the decisions.

## Then decide

By the end of the first month, you should be able to write one page: what's working and shouldn't be touched, what's broken and needs fixing first, and where the next pound should go.

That page is the start of your plan, and it's much easier to defend than a list of changes made in week two.
